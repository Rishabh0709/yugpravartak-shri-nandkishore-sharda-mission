A practice for the age

The Buddhi-Vivek Yoga Sadhana

The need of the age

A simple spiritual path for an intellectual age

Since the earliest times, in search of joy, peace, enthusiasm and happiness, human beings have taken up many practices — knowledge, devotion, selfless action, yoga, japa, tapa, mantra and meditation. History bears witness that the practices which proved effective for human welfare were those in tune with the thought, the needs and the knowledge of their time.

The rise of information technology in the 1970s laid the ground of an intellectual age. Today the steady growth of the intellect is the basis of material prosperity, and thought grounded in buddhi and vivek is the foundation of present and future progress. With this in view, after taking up the chakra in 1990, Bhaiyaji — understanding the importance of buddhi and vivek in the human civilisation to come — set out this practice.

This is an age-appropriate, meaningful practice that turns that same buddhi and vivek, naturally, towards spiritual progress.
What the practice is

Spiritual progress while living a worldly life

In the Buddhi-Vivek Yoga Sadhana, buddhi and vivek are not only shaped but also made pure. A person trained in this practice lives in love, happiness, peace and joy, and helps those they meet to grow in these qualities too.

Modern science has made life comfortable, yet unrest, stress and a loss of direction have grown. Children, the young and many with a spiritual interest are looking for a simple and practical path. For the welfare of humanity, Bhaiyaji, drawing on his own experience, set out this simple, timely method to raise both the physical and the conscious life.

This practice frees a person from loneliness, fear and stress. Thought changes, the way of seeing shifts, negative thinking slowly turns positive, focus grows, and the joy of love begins to rise.

Purity while living in the world, like the lotus

The whole of this practice is explained through the image of the lotus: even while in the world, free of attachment; even while acting, at peace; even while progressing, pure within.

A person uses material means as they are needed, but as buddhi becomes pure and vivek awakens, attachment to wealth, desire and fame lessens of itself — and alongside material success, love, peace and lasting joy come within easy reach.

The steps of the practice

Six steps: from knowledge to karma-sadhana

From knowledge, science and reflection — to resolve, surrender and mindful karma-sadhana.

Step one

Knowledge

Knowledge is not mere information, but the understanding of the cosmos, of science, of society, family, the body and the many aspects of life. It clears the darkness of ignorance and awakens discernment.

Step two

Science

Free of prejudice, to seek the truth through curiosity, testing, analysis and reason. This gives the capacity to weigh what is true.

Step three

Reflection

On the ground of knowledge and science, serious thought about purpose, gain and loss, and the many sides of a matter. Doubt clears, and a firm resolve towards the goal takes shape.

Step four

Resolve

After reflection, the firm decision to reach the chosen goal. In resolve, there is no alternative to reaching the goal.

Step five

Surrender

Complete trust in and surrender to one's chosen deity. To offer one's rights, ownership and responsibility to the deity, and to accept complete dependence upon them.

Step six

Karma-sadhana

To move forward on the path of karma-sadhana while changing one's thought. As buddhi becomes pure and vivek awakens, action is done in a spirit of love, joy and cooperation.

The experiences of the practice

Three experiences: love, peace and joy

Mature practice awakens a selfless love, an evenness of mind and a divine joy within.

First experience

Love

Love is the true nature of God. It means forming a selfless bond with God and with every living being. Through love, life fills with delight, and the weight of material longing lessens.

Second experience

Peace

Peace is the state in which a person is not shaken physically, mentally, emotionally or spiritually. They learn to live in evenness of mind and move towards a life free of attachment.

Third experience

Joy

With the rising of love, a person comes into harmony with God and begins to rest in a continuous joy. This is a divine experience beyond words — the highest state of the practice.

The source and the places of sadhana

Knowledge flowing from the Siddhapeeth, a living practice at Manidweep

The source of these principles is the Siddhapeeth. Here Shardaji did sadhana and received knowledge and guidance from Maa Tripurasundari. It is the sacred place where the divine energy of Bhaiyaji's sadhana remains fully present today.

Sadhana done at the Siddhapeeth is held to bear fruit quickly. The same is true of Manidweep. Through the Buddhi-Vivek Yoga Sadhana given by Bhaiyaji, a person can do sadhana simply, everywhere, at every time, in every circumstance, even while carrying out worldly work.

In the spiritual knowledge discussions held at Manidweep every Sunday evening, weekly guidance is given on bringing this practice into daily life.

“Through the Buddhi-Vivek Yoga Sadhana, may one carry oneself and others, along with material progress, towards immortality.”
