The philosophy behind the service

Education philosophy & guiding principles

The principles born from Bhaiya Shri Nandkishore Sharda's education philosophy, on which Manidweep's scholarships and education service are run — treating education as a right, sanskar as its foundation, and self-reliance as life's meaningful direction.

Bhaiyaji's education philosophy

Education is every person's right, and every talent's path

Education is complete only when it develops sanskar, humility and a sense of duty alongside knowledge.

Education — every person's right

At the root of Bhaiya Shri Nandkishore Sharda's education philosophy lay the belief that education is every person's fundamental right, and that financial hardship should never stand in the way of any girl's future. God has given every person some talent; education is the means to recognise that talent, develop it, and give life a meaningful direction — so it should not remain limited to the privileged, but reach every section of society.

Education and sanskar — complementary to each other

He held education and sanskar as complements to one another. To him, an educated person is one who, along with knowledge, is also humble, sensitive and dutiful. True education is that which advances a person's intellectual, moral, social and spiritual development together, and inspires them to recognise their inner strength and connect their life to a wider purpose.

Girls' education — the cornerstone of women's empowerment

He held girls' education as the cornerstone of women's empowerment. An educated and sanskar-rooted girl goes on to give her family direction, becomes her children's first teacher, and a responsible citizen of society.

Opportunity — tied to need, free of discrimination

With this same far-sightedness, scholarships were decided not by examination marks alone, but by keeping in view financial need, family circumstance and the real cost of education — with no discrimination on grounds of caste, religion or sect.

'For the good of all, for the happiness of all'

The guiding principles

Six principles, one whole vision of service

These principles keep the scholarship from remaining mere financial help — they make it a means of education, sanskar, personal growth and transparent service.

1

No one should be denied education

Financial hardship should never stand in the way of a girl's studies. Education support should be a means towards her academic, moral, intellectual, financial and social advancement.

2

Education is incomplete without sanskar

Girls should develop devotion to knowledge, dignity, self-confidence, self-respect, human sensitivity and compassion.

3

Rights should come with awareness of duty

Students should understand their duties alongside their rights. Along with an equal right to nature's resources, they should develop love and respect for nature.

4

Development should be whole and balanced

A positive coordination of mental, physical, intellectual and spiritual aspects, so that a girl's personality develops in a balanced and empowered way.

5

Members personally bear administrative costs

Administrative expenses such as telephone, postage, stationery and travel are borne personally by trust members, so that the trust's entire income can go towards scholarships.

6

Funds are kept secure, the interest goes to scholarships

Contributed funds are kept safe as fixed deposits in banks, and the entire interest earned is distributed among the girls as scholarships.

Registration documents, year-wise audited financial reports and scholarship certificates — all are publicly available on the transparency page.

From principle to service

Where support, sanskar and transparency together turn education into life-building

These principles bind the service work with clarity alongside compassion, accountability alongside equality, and character-building alongside scholarship. This same integrated vision went on to become the foundation of how the Swami Vivekanand Students' Welfare Charitable Trust operates.
