Why the Trust Began

Education was available in principle. For many girls, it remained unreachable in practice.

Across western Rajasthan, financial hardship, preference for sons and inadequate learning conditions repeatedly interrupted girls’ education. Bhaiyaji saw that paying a fee could prevent one immediate loss—but meaningful empowerment required education to be joined with character and the ability to stand independently.

A Conviction Becomes a Structure

Support that protects both education and self-respect

The Trust was established on 10 June 1996 under Bhaiyaji’s guiding vision, with Maa Basanti helping translate that conviction into organised service. Its purpose was not to create one more school, but to reach deserving girls across many schools and respond wherever financial difficulty threatened continuity.

From the beginning, assistance was offered as a rightful opportunity rather than an act of pity. This distinction remains central: a student should be able to receive help with confidence, concentrate on learning and imagine a future larger than her present circumstances.

The Trust does not see a beneficiary waiting to be rescued. It sees a capable young person whose path must not be closed by circumstances.

How Support Begins

Need is understood carefully, and assistance moves through a transparent path

Selection is based on genuine financial need—not marks, caste, community, religion or personal influence. Schools play an important role because teachers are often the first to recognise when a student is at risk of leaving education.

  1. 01
    A teacher recognises the difficulty

    The class teacher observes when family circumstances are placing a girl’s attendance, fees or continued schooling at risk.

  2. 02
    The school verifies the situation

    The Principal reviews the need and formally recommends the student, creating a responsible institutional starting point.

  3. 03
    The Trust considers the real requirement

    Support is aligned with the student’s educational circumstances rather than determined through rank, social identity or favour.

  4. 04
    Assistance reaches education directly

    Fees and learning support are routed through the institution wherever applicable, maintaining accountability and protecting the student’s dignity.

Beyond the Scholarship

The everyday work is a continuing relationship—not a once-a-year transaction

Financial support removes an immediate barrier. Regular contact, listening and guidance help a student build the confidence and practical understanding needed to use that opportunity well.

Sunday at Manidweep

Guidance that stays present as life changes

During the active class months, Sunday Sanskar and personality-development sessions bring students into sustained contact with mentors. Conversations cover study habits, time management, examination preparation, emotional pressures, health, relationships, rights and responsibilities.

Qualified volunteers and invited speakers help widen students’ awareness of educational and professional possibilities. When a student reaches a transition—another class, college, vocational learning or employment—the relationship makes it easier to seek direction rather than face the decision alone.

A Brief Historical Overview

What began as a response to interrupted schooling became a continuous circle of education and guidance.

When the Trust was established in 1996, its first concern was immediate and practical: a deserving girl should not leave school merely because her family could not meet the cost. Instead of limiting this effort to one institution, the Trust worked through existing schools so that verified need could be answered across a wider educational community.

As the years progressed, the relationship deepened beyond scholarship distribution. Academic follow-through, Sunday Sanskar classes, personal counselling and exposure to future possibilities became integral to the work. Bhaiyaji’s founding vision was carried forward by Maa Basanti and, since 2021, continues under Madhu Maa’s leadership—with the same emphasis on dignity, values and self-reliance.

Understanding the Trust

Questions families and well-wishers often ask

Clear answers are part of responsible service. These principles explain both the Trust’s focus and the way it protects every contribution.

Why did the Trust support students across existing schools?

Working across schools allows assistance to reach far more girls than a single institution could serve. The Trust can respond wherever verified need arises while students remain connected to their own learning environment.

Why is this Trust focused on girls?

It was created in response to the educational exclusion experienced by girls. Support for boys is organised separately through Gyanyogi Shri Nandkishore Sharda Adhyatm Kendra.

How are administrative expenses managed?

Members voluntarily bear the Trust’s administrative expenditure, allowing contributions received for education to remain directed toward educational support.

Can someone contribute without making a donation?

Yes. Time, mentorship, professional guidance and sincere voluntary service can all strengthen the circle around students.
