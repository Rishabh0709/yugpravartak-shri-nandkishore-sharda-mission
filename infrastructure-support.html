Looking Beyond the Scholarship

Bhaiyaji saw the entire environment surrounding a student

Financial assistance could help a girl remain enrolled, but it could not repair a crumbling classroom or create drinking water where none existed. Educational opportunity had to include the physical conditions in which learning took place.

From Observation to Action

He did not treat poor facilities as someone else’s problem

When Bhaiyaji encountered schools without adequate rooms, furniture, water or sanitation, he responded personally. He contributed funds, encouraged fellow members to participate and brought the needs of schools before district officials, elected representatives and other supporters.

The approach was practical and collaborative: understand what prevented students from learning with dignity, identify the facility that would make the greatest difference and bring willing people together to complete it.

Partnership with Purpose

Personal giving became collective responsibility

The Mission’s contribution often served as a catalyst. Members, public representatives and community supporters joined efforts so that schools could receive facilities beyond what one individual contribution might accomplish.

This created more than a building. It affirmed to students—particularly girls studying amid neglect and scarcity—that their education, safety and comfort mattered to the wider community.

A classroom is made of brick and furniture. A learning environment is also made of dignity, safety and the feeling that someone believes your future matters.
What the Support Created

Essential facilities that allow education to happen with dignity

01 · Space

Classrooms & halls

Additional teaching rooms and gathering spaces reduced constraints and gave students more functional places to learn.

02 · Health

Water & sanitation

Water-huts and toilets addressed basic health, safety and dignity—needs that directly affect attendance, especially for girls.

03 · Readiness

Furniture & laboratories

Desks, classroom furniture and computer facilities helped turn physical rooms into environments equipped for meaningful study.

Selected Contributions

A practical legacy across educational institutions in Jodhpur

The work took different forms according to the needs of each institution—from an individual classroom to a specialised academic block.

Reception hall and classrooms

The Shri Nandkishore Sharda Reception Hall and three classrooms were constructed at Mahalaxmi Girls’ School, Pratapnagar.

Commerce education

Financial support was provided for the Kumari Basanti Manihar Commerce Block at Mahila Mahavidyalaya, Jodhpur.

Tulsi water-huts

Drinking-water facilities were created in two girls’ schools in memory of Smt. Tulsi Devi.

Furniture for classrooms

Furniture was arranged for sixteen classrooms across seven girls’ schools, improving everyday learning conditions.

Computer laboratories

Technology facilities formed part of the wider effort to give students access to learning environments relevant to their future.

Safety and basic facilities

Boundary walls, toilets and other essential works helped schools become safer, healthier and more complete places of learning.
